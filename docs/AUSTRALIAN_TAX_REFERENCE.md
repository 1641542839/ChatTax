# 🇦🇺 Australian Tax Quick Reference

## 📋 Key Information for Developers

This document provides essential Australian tax information for developers working on ChatTax.

---

## 🏢 Australian Taxation Office (ATO)

**Official Website**: https://www.ato.gov.au/  
**myGov Portal**: https://my.gov.au/  
**Tax Help Program**: 13 28 61

The ATO is Australia's principal revenue collection agency. It administers:
- Income tax (individuals and businesses)
- GST (Goods and Services Tax)
- Superannuation
- Various other taxes and levies

---

## 📅 Important Dates (For Individual Taxpayers)

### Financial Year
**1 July to 30 June** (not calendar year!)
- Example: 2023-24 financial year = 1 July 2023 to 30 June 2024

### Key Deadlines
- **31 October**: Lodgement deadline for individuals lodging their own return
- **15 May (next year)**: Extended deadline if using a registered tax agent
- **21 October**: Payment due date (if lodging by 31 October)

### Quarterly Dates (for GST/BAS)
- Q1: July-September (due 28 October)
- Q2: October-December (due 28 February)
- Q3: January-March (due 28 April)
- Q4: April-June (due 28 July)

## 💰 Tax Rates (2023-24)

### Resident Tax Rates

| Taxable Income | Tax on this income |
|----------------|-------------------|
| $0 – $18,200 | Nil (Tax-free threshold) |
| $18,201 – $45,000 | 19c for each $1 over $18,200 |
| $45,001 – $120,000 | $5,092 plus 32.5c for each $1 over $45,000 |
| $120,001 – $180,000 | $29,467 plus 37c for each $1 over $120,000 |
| $180,001 and above | $51,667 plus 45c for each $1 over $180,000 |

**Medicare Levy**: 2% of taxable income (additional to above rates)

### Foreign Resident Tax Rates
Foreign residents are NOT entitled to the tax-free threshold.

| Taxable Income | Tax on this income |
|----------------|-------------------|
| $0 – $120,000 | 32.5c for each $1 |
| $120,001 – $180,000 | $39,000 plus 37c for each $1 over $120,000 |
| $180,001 and above | $61,200 plus 45c for each $1 over $180,000 |

### Working Holiday Maker Tax Rates
Special rates apply for working holiday makers (417 and 462 visas).

| Taxable Income | Tax on this income |
|----------------|-------------------|
| $0 – $45,000 | 15c for each $1 |
| $45,001 – $120,000 | $6,750 plus 32.5c for each $1 over $45,000 |
| $120,001 – $180,000 | $31,125 plus 37c for each $1 over $120,000 |
| $180,001 and above | $53,325 plus 45c for each $1 over $180,000 |

## 📄 Common Income Types

### PAYG (Pay As You Go)
- Salary/wages from employment
- Tax withheld by employer
- **Payment Summary** (formerly "group certificate") shows:
  - Gross income
  - Tax withheld
  - Employer details

### Business Income
- Sole trader income
- Partnership distributions
- ABN (Australian Business Number) required
- BAS (Business Activity Statement) reporting

### Investment Income
- **Dividends**: From Australian shares (may include franking credits)
- **Interest**: Bank accounts, term deposits
- **Capital Gains**: From selling assets (50% discount if held >12 months)
- **Rental Income**: From investment properties

### Government Payments
- Centrelink payments (JobSeeker, Age Pension, etc.)
- Some are taxable, some are not

### Superannuation
- Employer contributions (Superannuation Guarantee - 11% in 2023-24)
- Personal contributions (concessional/non-concessional)
- Early access in limited circumstances

## 🧾 Common Deductions

### Work-Related Expenses
- **Car Expenses**: Cents per km method or logbook method
- **Home Office**: Fixed rate method (67c/hour in 2023-24) or actual expenses
- **Clothing**: Only uniforms, protective clothing, occupation-specific
- **Self-Education**: Course must relate to current employment
- **Travel**: Work-related travel (not home to work)
- **Tools & Equipment**: Work-related tools, computers, phones
- **Union Fees**: Trade union membership fees
- **Professional Memberships**: Industry associations, licenses

### Other Deductions
- **Investment Property Expenses**: Repairs, interest, agent fees, insurance
- **Investment Management Costs**: Financial advisor fees
- **Donations**: To DGRs (Deductible Gift Recipients) only - $2+ for tax receipt
- **Tax Agent Fees**: Cost of preparing your tax return

### What You CANNOT Claim
- Private expenses (meals, personal clothing)
- Travel between home and work
- Fines and penalties
- Income protection insurance premiums (different from super)

## 🎯 Tax Offsets (Tax Credits)

### Low Income Tax Offset (LITO)
- Up to $700 for incomes below $66,668
- Phases out completely at $66,667

### Low and Middle Income Tax Offset (LMITO)
- **Removed from 2023-24** - was available until 2021-22

### Senior Australians and Pensioners Tax Offset (SAPTO)
- For seniors meeting age and income tests
- Up to $2,230 (single) or $1,602 (each partner)

### Private Health Insurance Rebate
- Reduces cost of private health insurance
- Based on income tier
- Can claim as premium reduction or tax offset

### Franking Credits
- Attached to Australian dividends
- Represents company tax already paid
- Refundable if tax payable is less than credit

## 🔐 myGov Account

**Essential for all taxpayers**

### What is myGov?
- Online portal to access Australian government services
- Link to ATO, Centrelink, Medicare, etc.
- Required to lodge tax return online

### Setting Up
1. Create myGov account at https://my.gov.au/
2. Verify identity (100 points check)
3. Link ATO to myGov
4. Set up security (password, security questions, SMS code)

### Features
- Lodge tax return online
- View pre-filled information
- Check refund status
- Manage payment plans
- Correspondence with ATO

## 📱 ATO Online Services

### ATO App
- Mobile app for accessing tax information
- Check refund status
- Update contact details
- View notices

### Pre-fill Service
- ATO automatically receives information from:
  - Employers (payment summaries)
  - Banks (interest earned)
  - Private health insurers
  - Government agencies
- Available from mid-July each year
- Verify before lodging (can take 2-3 weeks to fully populate)

## 🏠 Residency for Tax Purposes

**CRITICAL**: Different from visa/citizenship status

### Australian Resident for Tax Purposes
You are generally a resident if you:
- Reside in Australia
- Physically present in Australia for more than 183 days
- Have domicile in Australia

**Implications**:
- Tax on worldwide income
- Access to tax-free threshold ($18,200)
- Medicare levy applies

### Foreign Resident for Tax Purposes
You are generally a foreign resident if you:
- Live overseas permanently
- Working temporarily in Australia (<183 days)

**Implications**:
- Tax on Australian-sourced income only
- NO tax-free threshold (32.5% from first dollar)
- NO Medicare levy
- Different tax rates

### Working Holiday Maker
Special category for 417 and 462 visa holders:
- 15% tax rate up to $45,000
- Then standard rates apply
- Still pay Medicare levy

## 📊 Special Situations

### HECS-HELP Debt
- Student loan repayment
- Compulsory once income exceeds threshold ($51,550 in 2023-24)
- Repayment rates: 1% to 10% of income
- Automatically calculated and withheld from salary

### First Home Super Saver (FHSS) Scheme
- Withdraw voluntary super contributions for first home deposit
- Max $15,000 per year, $50,000 total
- Must meet eligibility criteria

### Superannuation
- **Superannuation Guarantee**: 11% employer contribution (2023-24)
- **Concessional cap**: $27,500 per year
- **Non-concessional cap**: $110,000 per year
- Taxed at 15% on entry (concessional)
- Taxed at 0% on exit (after age 60, in retirement)

### Capital Gains Tax (CGT)
- Applies to assets acquired after 20 September 1985
- **Main residence exempt** (your home)
- **50% discount** if held for more than 12 months
- Calculated on sale date, not settlement

## 🌏 States and Territories

Australia has 8 states/territories (some have state taxes):

1. **NSW** - New South Wales
2. **VIC** - Victoria
3. **QLD** - Queensland
4. **WA** - Western Australia
5. **SA** - South Australia
6. **TAS** - Tasmania
7. **NT** - Northern Territory

**State taxes** (not handled in personal tax return):
- Stamp duty (property purchases)
- Land tax (investment properties)
- Payroll tax (employers)

## 🔍 Common Mistakes to Avoid

### In Code
1. ❌ Using calendar year instead of financial year
2. ❌ Referring to "IRS" instead of "ATO"
3. ❌ Using US tax forms (W-2, 1099) - use "payment summary"
4. ❌ Implementing business tax logic (we only do personal tax)
5. ❌ Using "filing status" (married/single) - Australia is individual-based

### In Tax Logic
1. ❌ Claiming home-to-work travel (not deductible)
2. ❌ Forgetting Medicare levy (2%)
3. ❌ Using wrong tax rates for foreign residents
4. ❌ Ignoring franking credits on dividends
5. ❌ Claiming non-DGR donations

- No dependents
- No investments

1. **Financial Year**: Always 1 July to 30 June
